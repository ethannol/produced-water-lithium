PRODUCED-WATER LITHIUM
Where is lithium in U.S. oil and gas produced water, and how much of that picture is an artifact of what was sampled, measured and compiled?

This project is the feedstock question for produced-water lithium, done with the same honesty.

Central question, two parts:

1. Descriptive: which U.S. basins and formations have produced-water lithium concentrations and major-ion chemistry that would support extraction, with uncertainty, and how much does that ranking change when the known biases of the compilation are accounted for?
2. Predictive: where lithium was not measured, how well can it be predicted from major-ion chemistry, depth and formation, when the evaluation holds out whole source datasets and whole basins rather than random rows?

Do not decide the answers in advance. "The ranking is robust" and "the ranking is mostly an artifact of which datasets were compiled" are both valid findings. "Lithium cannot be usefully predicted out of basin" is a valid finding. The portfolio's value is the quality of the investigation.

KNOWN TRAPS, TO BE EXPOSED NOT ASSUMED

- Compilation bias: datasets were added because they were lithium-rich. A basin's apparent lithium level partly reflects which papers were compiled. Show the ranking with and without the datasets the version history names as lithium-focused, and by dataset within basin.
- Missing is not random: lithium was measured where someone cared about lithium. Rows with Li present differ systematically from rows without. Quantify it (compare TDS, depth, basin mix, dataset, decade between the two groups).
- Dataset batch effects: the same formation measured by two sources can differ by method, era, and unit conversion. IDDB is the batch variable; use it in the evaluation.
- Duplicates and time series: some datasets are repeated samples from the same well. The well, or the well-dataset pair, is the unit, not the row. Make the deduplication explicit and checked.
- Unit and density: ppm-to-mg/L conversion used specific gravity that was itself sometimes estimated. Report how many rows depended on an estimated SG.
- Charge balance: CHARGEBAL exists; samples far out of balance are suspect. Pre-register a tolerance and report how many rows it removes, and the sensitivity of the ranking to it.
- Approximate coordinates: COORDAPX flags approximate locations. Do not draw spatial conclusions finer than the coordinate quality supports.
- Geothermal and groundwater rows: PLAYTYPE and WELLTYPE include non-produced-water samples. Decide what counts and say so.

VIABILITY DEFINITION

Pre-register a screening definition before looking at the Li distribution by basin, and cite the sources for the thresholds. Extraction screening commonly uses a lithium concentration floor and a magnesium-to-lithium ratio ceiling because magnesium competes in direct lithium extraction, with total dissolved solids as a further constraint. Pick a primary threshold set with a citation (published screening studies of produced-water lithium and DLE process papers; find and cite at least two), and a sensitivity grid around it. Report, per basin and per FORMSIMPLE: number of wells with Li measured, median and interquartile Li, fraction of wells above the floor, Mg/Li distribution, and a bootstrap interval that resamples wells, not rows, clustered by dataset where a basin has more than one. Rank basins with the uncertainty shown; do not publish a point ranking.

PREDICTIVE MODEL

Target: log lithium, on wells where it was measured and detected. Features: major-ion chemistry transformed appropriately (log concentrations or log-ratios over the major-ion subcomposition; say which and why), TDS, depth, and optionally formation age or play type. Two models: a regularized linear model and a gradient boosted tree model (scikit-learn), small fixed grid inside the training folds. No deep learning.

Evaluate under three protocols: random k-fold by well; leave-one-dataset-out (IDDB); leave-one-basin-out. Report RMSE on log Li, R-squared, and, for the screening use, classification quality against the pre-registered floor (AUC, precision at the floor, calibration). The headline figure is the out-of-sample error under the three protocols side by side with spread. If the model works within a basin and fails across basins, say so plainly; that is the useful finding for someone deciding whether to sample a new field.

Include a positive-control check: predict TDS from the same features to show the pipeline can learn something that is genuinely predictable.
